## 4. Recommendations by expert hat

### Trader / stock-market expert
- **The overnight edge is thin and statistically weak.** Your own notes: adopted exit rule **t = 1.5, "mostly 2025"**, and backtests show **red (negative)** numbers across most scenarios (₹1,269/night optimised vs ₹707 equal on ₹5 L, top-5). That is a small, fragile edge after ~23 bps round-trip costs. Before scaling capital, I would demand: (a) a **forward/paper track of ≥ 30–60 live nights** with the *real* exit rule and *measured* slippage, not backtest slippage; (b) **regime conditioning** (the t-stat is dominated by 2025 — test 2023–24 and 2026 separately); (c) a **pre-registered kill criterion** (e.g. stop if realised Sharpe < X over Y nights) so you don't nurse a losing trade.
- **Concentration risk.** Top-5 of a universe with no price floor and T2T/ASM exclusions = high idiosyncratic + gap risk overnight (you can't exit a gap-down in a pre-open auction the way you think — the auction *is* the exit and it can gap against you). Add a **per-stock and total overnight exposure cap** and a **correlated-name cap** (don't hold 5 large-caps in one sector).
- **Exit-rule fragility.** "Limit-sell at open + 75% to predicted high, else 12:00 market" has two failure modes: the limit never fills (you're stuck) and the 12:00 market dump. Consider a **time-stop + adverse-move stop** hybrid and backtest the *fill* assumption (does the limit actually fill? your `fill.low_d50 ≈ 0.67` says ~⅓ of the time the price doesn't reach your level).
- **The live swing trader is on a real account (₹4,313, LIVE mode) with every strategy disabled** ("no backtested edge"). That's the right *discipline* — but it means the only live thing you have is a **1-share paper-ish swing book**. Be explicit about which strategies have a backtested edge and gate live trading on that, or you're paying real spread/slippage for no edge.

### Risk manager
- **Single machine = single point of failure.** One PX13 hosts data, ML, execution, and the only Fyers token. If it dies, sleeps, or the token lapses at 09:15, you have no position management. At minimum: (a) a **heartbeat/watchdog** that alerts (Telegram) if the session/token/data-update hasn't run by T+X minutes; (b) a **recovery runbook** (you have `docs/07-operations/backup-and-recovery.md` — make it a tested one-click); (c) consider a cheap always-on box (Raspberry Pi / VPS) that *only* monitors + can send a kill/flatten order.
- **Fail-closed is good; fail-*loud* is missing.** Your static-IP fail-closed design is excellent. But right now a dead token just produces an empty env var and a log line. Add **active alerting** on: token failure, data-update failure, at-close missing, session not-up, and any `OrderUncertain`.
- **Secrets hygiene.** Fyers secrets were exposed in chat (rotate now); Shoonya creds sit in plain user env vars; DPAPI-encrypted `fyers_secrets.bin` is good. Move toward a single secrets store + never log, and add a `.gitignore`/manifest check so `state\` and `.env` never ship in zips (`files.zip`, `nse-algo-v3.11.zip` are at the root — verify they don't contain `state\`/tokens).

### Data scientist / ML
- **Deliver the best model.** ALT-LINK is your best (highest rank IC, beat reference on close-to-close and open-to-close) yet it's not in `latest.csv`. Wire it into the nightly set and **A/B the portfolio** on ALT vs ALT-LINK for a month before declaring a winner — your keep/discard rule (beat reference on ≥ 8 of 12 quarters without worse pinball) is sound; enforce it automatically.
- **Quantile calibration & the DeepLOB contribution.** Your fold table shows DeepLOB is *barely* adding value (e.g. `y_v` pinball worse in **12/12** folds; `y_r` rank IC higher in only 5/12). That suggests the blend is mostly LightGBM. Run an ablation: (a) LGBM-only, (b) +DeepLOB, (c) +links, and keep only the blocks that beat the reference on your keep/discard rule. Don't ship complexity that isn't paying.
- **Nowcast is your highest-leverage feature.** The 15:37 delivery-% nowcast is the thing that separates ALT-CLOSE from a stale model. Invest in its out-of-sample yearly walk-forward and in *how far* it moves the ranking (a feature-importance / SHAP study on the top-5 picks, not just IC).
- **Lookahead discipline is already good** (`tests\test_lookahead.py`, frozen `link_map.json`, N-1 alt data). Keep it. Add a **data-freshness assertion** to every `predict` (fail if `raw_ohlcv` / `at_close` / `preopen` for the target date is missing or stale) so a silent no-op never masquerades as a forecast.
- **Feature store duplication.** `features_output_clean.parquet` (1.87 GB) sits in DATA, ML has its own `dataset_v2`, and ALGO has `features.py`/`features_v1_3_optimized.py`. One canonical feature store (in DATA, versioned by the contract) would remove drift risk between engines.

### Finance / quant
- **Model costs, don't assume them.** You use Shoonya delivery ≈ 23.3 bps and intraday ≈ 9.2 bps as constants. For the overnight trade, the *real* cost is the **auction fill** — model the distribution of your limit fill vs the auction (you have pre-open recordings now), and the **opportunity cost of a non-fill**. A 23 bps assumption can flatter a 0.4% edge.
- **Capacity & market impact.** You're sizing as a share of daily volume (post-close 0.032% / pre-open 0.259% median) — good instinct. But the overnight gap trade in *small, illiquid* names (no price floor) has **non-linear impact**: your backtest slippage (₹431→₹280) is a scalar. Stress-test with impact ∝ √(participation) and cap participation at 1–2% of the session volume for the names you actually pick.
- **Sharpe is not the right headline for a 1-night horizon.** Report **per-night expectancy (₹), hit-rate, max adverse excursion overnight, and the distribution of the worst 5 nights**, not an annualised Sharpe that a 1-day hold can't support. Your own "red minus figures" in the workbook are the honest number — keep leading with them.
- **Benchmark the edge.** A gap-up long strategy in a rising 2025 market can look great and be beta. Run it **long/flat vs a Nifty-500 gap-up basket of equal size** to prove the *selection* adds value, not the *market*.

### Developer / engineer
- **The token is a single point of failure with no retry-to-live.** The 3×/2-min retry is fine for network, but a *credential* error should **page you**, not retry for 6 minutes and die. Add a distinct "fatal vs transient" classification + Telegram alert.
- **Task configuration is inconsistent** (see #2). Centralise task registration in one script that enforces: `Password` logon, `WakeToRun`, `StartWhenAvailable`, `StopIfGoingOnBatteries=False`, a sane `ExecutionTimeLimit`, and a **post-run self-check** (the task's own `.bat` should `exit /b 1` on any non-zero child so the scheduler records the failure — several of your failures are masked by `cmd /c` swallowing exit codes).
- **`cmd /c` is hiding real failures.** Multiple tasks (PreOpen, AtClose, MinuteArchive, Predict) wrap a `.bat` in `cmd /c`. If the Python inside returns non-zero but the `.bat` doesn't propagate it, the scheduler shows success. Add `exit /b %ERRORLEVEL%` (or `call`) in every `.bat`, and verify by forcing a failure.
- **Observability.** You have `monitor.json`, `netcheck`, `doctor`, and a desk GUI — good. Add a single **daily "system health" rollup** (token OK? data-update OK? at-close present? predictions present? session up?) emailed/Telegram'd at 09:05. That one report would have caught every red in §2.
- **CI.** You have `test_docs.py`, lookahead tests, and 9 sizing unit tests. Run them in a scheduled nightly job (or on every `run_*.bat`) so a regression fails loudly instead of in production.

### Architect
- **The design is genuinely good and I'd keep the bones:** one engine for every mode (backtest/forward/live share code), broker-agnostic adapters, a **data contract as the single source of truth**, fail-closed static-IP, ADRs + docs-as-code, and measured GPU/CPU dispatch. That's more mature than most retail quant stacks. Don't rebuild it.
- **The weak spot is the *integration & availability* layer, not the algorithms.** Four engines + a token + a scheduler on one laptop, with file-based state and no orchestration/monitoring, is where you're losing (the §2 reds are all integration/availability failures, not model failures). Add a thin **orchestrator/monitor** (even a small Python service) that: knows the daily DAG (token → pre-open → data → predict → size → exit), enforces freshness, alerts on failure, and can trigger the runbook. You already have the DAG documented in `MASTER_CONTEXT.md` — encode it.
- **Decouple the token from the data path where possible.** Pre-open and at-close are *your own* Fyers usage; a token outage shouldn't be able to silently produce an empty `at_close\`. Add "expected-but-missing" detection at the consumer.
- **State sharing via files is fine at this scale** but add **schema/version guards** at every consumer (the contract validator exists — call it in ML/ALGO on read, not just in DATA).

### Visionary / researcher
- **You're under-using the 128 GB + GPU for *research*, which is where they pay.** The PX13 doc is right that GPU won't speed up live ticks but will crush batched research. Priorities I'd fund next: (a) **whole-universe depth replay** (`book-study` on a week of TBT/Upstox 30-level recordings) to find a *measured* microstructure edge, since the thresholds are currently "literature + common sense"; (b) **cross-sectional ML on the minute archive** (899 stocks, 2018→) — you have the data, it's just 455/899; (c) **a regime-conditional overnight model** (HMM regime already exists in ALGO — feed it into the ML as a feature).
- **The alt-data moat is real but unproven.** Short-selling + MTF are backfilled now; the open question "do they have predictive value?" is the difference between a commodity gap-trade and an edge. Run the ALT vs original A/B *on the portfolio PnL*, not just IC.
- **Don't over-build the exit; under-invest in *selection*.** The most valuable next research (from your own to-do list) is the **flexible exit** (hold vs sell, decided by next-day ML + gap + delivery + auction behaviour) — that's where a 1-night trade becomes a 1–3 day trade and the capacity/edge both improve. It's also where the DeepLOB/nowcast features actually earn their keep.

### CPU note (your original question — resolved)
The "stuck at 600 MHz" was a **power-state / driver P-state latch** on the Strix-Point APU, cleared by the reboot. It's not a config bug in your trading stack. If it recurs: confirm you're on AC + the 100 W+ charger, set **MyASUS/Armoury Crate → Performance**, update the BIOS + AMD chipset driver, and keep **HWiNFO64** handy to watch `Package Power (W)` and `Tj` under a `nsealgo gpu`/walk-forward load — that tells you immediately whether you're power-capped (low W, low °C) or thermally capped (high °C). For the batched research workloads you actually run, a few percent of P-state won't matter; the GPU is where the throughput lives.
